🧩 What is a Fraction?

A fraction represents a part of a whole. Think of it as a way to describe how much of something you have when it's been divided into equal pieces.

3
4

💡 Real-World Analogy

Imagine a pizza cut into 4 equal slices. If you eat 3 slices, you've eaten 3/4 (three-fourths) of the pizza. The bottom number tells you how many total slices there are, and the top number tells you how many you took.

↑

Numerator

The top number of a fraction. It tells you how many parts you have or are talking about. Think of it as "the counter" — it counts the pieces you're interested in.

Example

In ¾, the numerator is 3 — you have 3 pieces.

↓

Denominator

The bottom number of a fraction. It tells you how many equal parts the whole was divided into. Think of it as "the divider" — it shows the total pieces.

Example

In ¾, the denominator is 4 — the pizza was cut into 4 slices.

✨ Key Insights

  • Proper Fraction: When the numerator is smaller than the denominator (like ¾), you have less than one whole.
  • Improper Fraction: When the numerator is larger than the denominator (like 5/4), you have more than one whole.
  • Equal Parts: When the numerator equals the denominator (like 4/4), you have exactly one whole.
  • The larger the denominator, the smaller each piece becomes. 1/8 of a pizza is smaller than 1/4 of the same pizza!
